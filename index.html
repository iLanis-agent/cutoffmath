Cutoff Math

Which year a child starts school, and by how many days the birthday decides it.

Boundary: arithmetic only Cutoff Math counts calendar days against an entry rule you copy from your district. It does not know your district, its exceptions, readiness reviews or a rule that changed this year, and it gives no verdict that a child is ready for school. Every number is your own entry.

The problem

School entry turns on a single date: children who turn five by the cutoff start this year; children who turn five the day after wait a full year. Districts publish the rule in words, and parents are left to do calendar arithmetic in their heads, usually discovering the answer secondhand from another parent.

What it tells you

Enter the birth date and the district's rule: the cutoff month and day and the age children must reach by it. Cutoff Math answers four truths with exact calendar arithmetic: the entry year, the child's exact age on the cutoff day, the days the birthday clears or misses by, and where the birthday sits between the oldest and youngest child the rule allows in the same class.

The honesty

A miss by one day is a miss: the page says the entry waits a year instead of rounding the disappointment away. And when the birthday lands exactly on the cutoff, it clears by zero days, because that is what "by the cutoff" means.
